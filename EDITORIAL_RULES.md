# Editorial rules — brwncltr

These rules apply to research notes, drafts, scores, social captions, and any future CMS fields. They are **not** optional for agents.

Human review is required before publication. Agents never publish.

---

## Accuracy: do not invent

**Never fabricate** quotes, facts, statistics, dates, locations, titles, chart positions, box-office figures, follower counts, “reactions,” consensus, or sourcing.

If a number, quote, or date is not in a recorded source, it does not go in the draft. Paraphrase is allowed only when it does not add claims the source does not support.

Placeholders such as `[UNVERIFIED]`, `[NEED SOURCE]`, or status `BLOCKED` are required when something important is missing. Inventing a plausible detail to “keep the draft moving” is a rules violation.

---

## Reporting vs interpretation

- **Reporting:** what can be attributed to a source (documents, on-the-record speech, named reporting).
- **Interpretation:** what it might mean, why it resonates, what is in dispute.

Label analysis and opinion. Do not present the writer’s reading as an established fact. Do not bury speculation in a news lede.

---

## Sources

**Prefer primary sources** when they exist: the person’s own words, official notices, filings, raw video of an event, rights-holder release pages, original papers.

Secondary journalism is allowed and often necessary. **Attribute factual claims derived from other reporting** (publication + reporter if bylined + date). Do not launder one outlet’s exclusive as unsourced common knowledge.

Anonymous or off-record material: do not use unless a human editor has a policy and has approved it. Default for agents: **do not invent anonymity**; do not claim “sources say.”

---

## Social media

A public post can establish **that this account posted this text or media at approximately this time** (record URL, date/time, and whether the account is verified or merely claimed).

A post is **not** automatically evidence that:

- a broader group believes the same thing
- an event happened as described
- a statistic is true
- “everyone” is reacting

Quote social posts as posts. Verify viral claims **before repeating them** in the voice of the article. If verification fails, either omit, or report the claim *as a circulating claim* with what could and could not be checked—and mark remaining gaps.

---

## Viral claims

Default stance: **unverified until checked**.

- Trace to the earliest primary.
- Note what fact-checkers or original reporters found, with attribution.
- Do not repeat defamatory or medical claims “for color.”
- If the story *is* the virality, the piece must still separate **circulation** from **truth**.

---

## South Asian specificity (non-negotiable)

Do not flatten South Asian communities into one culture, one diaspora, one politics, or one aesthetic.

When relevant, **preserve distinctions** among:

- countries and regions
- religions and secular identities
- languages and scripts
- caste and class (only with care, sourcing, and without treating them as spice)
- migration waves and legal statuses
- urban/rural and island/mainland contexts
- named communities (e.g. specific ethnic or linguistic groups)

If the draft could replace every proper noun with “South Asian” and still “work,” it is too flat. Rewrite or `BLOCKED` for missing specificity.

Do not use India as a silent default for the subcontinent. Do not use “Desi” as a substitute for a more precise term when precision matters and is knowable.

---

## Harm, sensationalism, and controversy

Do not **manufacture controversy** or sensationalize for clicks. Headlines must be supportable by the piece.

Avoid:

- implied criminality or scandal without sourced facts
- sexualized or dehumanizing framing of communities
- trauma as decoration
- “secret war on…” constructions without evidence

Crime, communal violence, and suicide: follow a human editor’s later policy; until then, agents **do not** draft those topics without explicit owner authorization.

---

## Attribution and quotes

- Quotes must be **exact** or clearly marked as edited (ellipses, light cleanup of ums) per a future style guide; until then, **exact** and sourced.
- Do not “smooth” a quote into a better sentence.
- Translations: note original language and who translated (including “staff translation”) when the meaning is load-bearing.

---

## Human review and publication

- Nothing goes live without a human.
- Future WordPress jobs, if any, create **drafts** only after `APPROVED`.
- Agents do not email sources, impersonate staff, or scrape login-walled private content.

---

## Corrections mindset

If research finds that an earlier note in this repo was wrong, **do not silently overwrite** a `READY_FOR_REVIEW` or `APPROVED` packet. Flag the error in notes and wait for editor instruction (see [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md)).
