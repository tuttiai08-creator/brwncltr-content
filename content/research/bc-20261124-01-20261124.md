### 1c. What it mapped, and concrete scene details

**FACT, toilets, contemporaneous (strongest scene).** TOI, Oct. 5, 2010: "Chennai city has only 600 public toilets!" With each of 10 zones "supporting a population of around 4 lakh this is disturbing, say a few young, urban planners who are out to make public information available to the common man through TransparentChennai.com."

Raman, as quoted: "This means that many in the city don't have access to sanitation. We also found that the location of the toilets had very little relationship to slums in the area, residents of which would be most likely to use them. In one area we found many on the main road, of course they could be where slums existed earlier. But we also found that several were not used everyday."

The project found some zones had 100 toilets, others barely 20. "Enquiries with the corporation revealed there were 20 toilets in Zone 4, comprising the areas between Villivakkam, Ayanavaram, Sembium and Perambur, whereas a RTI application disclosed 40 toilets."

Sethuraman, as quoted: "In Zone 4, more men than women were using toilets. The users were 70% male and 30% female, and in some places women's latrines were used by men as women refused to come. Such information threw up questions about how sanitation for women is still out of their reach, despite the government's efforts."

The site gave counts by zone, plus "a detailed map of locations and user details for 20 toilets in Zone 4." Clicking a toilet showed "the results of the user, caretaker and observation survey." Data "updated till September." Data collected through RTI applications and questionnaires. (TOI, Oct. 5, 2010)

**FACT, Ward 176 audit, 2011.** The Hindu, Oct. 19, 2011: Volunteers presented findings on civic amenities in Ward 176 at the Indira Nagar Youth Hostel, as part of a "Ward Accountability Experiment." Issues: public sanitation, pedestrian walkability, surface garbage. Raman, as quoted: "Basically our goal is to create the data needed for holding these elected representatives accountable and I think that this is an experiment and a first step towards doing that."

A sidebar describes student volunteers Sophia and Venkatesh (Madras School of Social Work, second year) on Besant Nagar 31st Cross Street "armed with a plethora of maps, images printed out from Google Earth, survey sheets, measuring tape, and sketch pens," measuring footpath widths, tallies of footfalls and traffic, then moving to 7th Cross Street.

Siddharth Hande's presentation, as reported: Ward 176 "Contained 11 public toilets, nine of which were unusable. In fact, one of the toilets, damaged by the tsunami in 2004, had not yet been repaired." "Out of the 20 roads surveyed, only three were deemed walk-able and safe for pedestrians." "Out of 111 dumpsters, only 70 were found to be usable... And a whopping 46 roads in the area did not have dumpsters at all."

**FACT, Kalyanapuram / Buckingham Canal, 2013 (later than founding, very concrete).** LiveMint, Nov. 1, 2013: Kalyanapuram, a slum on the Buckingham Canal in northern Chennai, "1,000 families, each living in 150 sq. ft houses." At the entrance, a toilet complex. Thenappan G., 57, a coolie worker, "pays Rs 2 for using the toilet, which was refurbished in June." "You don't know what you were missing until you have it," he says. Before: cleaned irregularly; "People had to carry water as there was no running supply." After: "well-lit, laid with pink and white tiles, with iron doors that can be latched from inside. There is running water in each cubicle and they are cleaned every night." Five toilet complexes in the slum; four (three cubicles each) still dilapidated. The refurbished one, the largest, "was in a similar condition until the intervention of Transparent Chennai in 2012."

Mint's later counts (2013, not 2010): "Chennai has only 714 public toilets for a population of 4.6 million, which means that each seat is used by around 6,442 people - the National Urban Sanitation Policy sets a limit of 60 persons per seat." Mapped "a total of 134 toilets in 27 wards till now - of these, 78 toilets were mapped in 13 wards this year." Kalyanapuram was "their first live project." In six months, the story says, the settlement "built drains in every street, put in place a garbage disposal system, erected a wall to protect the burial ground premises and built a fortified area for women to wash clothes." Ward councillor named: Aavin R. Arulvel.

Do not collapse the 2010 "600 toilets" figure and the 2013 "714 toilets / 6,442 people per seat" figure into one number. Date each.

**FACT, bus routes.** TNIE, Nov. 6, 2026: "Under her, the team also created an easy-to-search bus route map for Chennai for the first time in 2010, which became a hit among residents. Raja said a dedicated team travelled on MTC buses with GPS devices to build it. At a time when Google Maps did not offer today's features, people could search for a starting point and destination to find the relevant bus numbers. The map also carried information on public toilets, accident hotspots and the locations of MRTS and Metro stations." Another initiative, "according to Somya, allowed residents to map the locations of potholes in their city."
- The GPS-on-MTC-buses detail is **single-source** (TNIE, attributed to Prabu Raja). A 2010 Hindu transport column on poor MTC route information (Ajai Sreevatsan, Aug. 10, 2010, https://www.thehindu.com/news/cities/chennai/Access-to-information-on-public-transit-systems-inadequate/article15777840.ece) does not mention Transparent Chennai and does not corroborate the GPS field work. Do not use "first time" or the GPS ride without the TNIE attribution; prefer a second source before putting the GPS ride in the lede.
- LAT and IE confirm a bus-route database / documented bus routes, without the GPS-ride color.

**FACT, Raman on the point of the work.** LAT, Nov. 2, 2026: "It was all following the bug of, like, how do we make cities meet their promise?" Raman said of their work.

**FACT, Raman and Shekhar in Mint, Nov. 1, 2013.** Raman: "We are collecting and using information that was previously unavailable to empower citizens to push for change, and also increase accountability of government officials in the process." Shekhar, separately: "We don't create the change, we are the vehicle for it. At the end of the day, the implementation of the findings is entirely in the ward councillor's hands who chooses what he wants to do with the data." Do not merge the two speakers.

### F1. Is there an exact count of how many of Chennai's ~600 public toilets were in or near slums?

**None exists, citywide.** Transparent Chennai's own work never states "X of 600" or "X of 714" toilets were in or near slums or the poorest neighborhoods. Raman's TOI line (Oct. 5, 2010) is qualitative: locations had "very little relationship to slums in the area." The 2011 issue brief and Sethuraman's Hindu op-ed (Apr. 27, 2012) say the same after the Zone 4 overlay: "many slum areas did not have a toilet nearby, and that there were clusters of toilets where there were no slums at all." Do not invent a citywide slum-adjacent total, and do not misuse the 85% figure below as a location count.

**What "600" is, versus TC's own later counts.** TOI's "only 600 public toilets" (Oct. 5, 2010) is the early public figure, with some zones at 100 and others "barely 20." Transparent Chennai's own 2011 issue brief, and Sethuraman's 2012 Hindu op-ed, give two citywide numbers from the team's own collection, neither of them 600:

- 572 toilets, from lists "sourced directly from" the ten zonal offices (voluntary).
- 714 toilets, from RTI replies, "separately from each zonal office." The issue brief treats 714 as the figure to use: "According to the city's responses to our RtI, Chennai only has a total of 714 public toilets," for a population of 46.81 lakhs (Census 2011 provisional totals). Every zone's RTI number differed from its voluntary list.

The Hindu news story, Sept. 15, 2011, also uses 714 for the metropolis. LiveMint (Nov. 1, 2013) later repeats 714 / 6,442 people per seat. In copy, date each number. Do not write "600, of which X were in slums."

**The closest exact figures that do exist are from the Zone 4 physical survey, not a citywide slum count.**

The issue brief: of the 714, "Transparent Chennai mapped and surveyed the 49 toilets in Zone 4." Methodology: the team "visited both the Corporation of Chennai (CoC), as well as all ten zonal offices" for counts and locations, then filed an RTI to corroborate; they then mapped and surveyed one zone, Zone 4, "to capture basic quality parameters, including presence of lights, water, electricity, structural integrity, cleanliness and others, and also conducted interviews with users and the caretaker at each toilet. Each toilet location was geo-referenced using GPS units, and close-up photographs of the amenities inside each toilet were taken." So yes: the team physically went to the toilets in Zone 4, used GPS, interviewed users and caretakers, and "field staff" scored cleanliness. The issue brief does not name the field staff. Sethuraman is "lead researcher" (The Hindu, Sept. 15, 2011). A search extract of Raman's JoCI 2012 paper adds that "Meryl" was sent to visit each of the ten zonal offices to compile the citywide list, and that the Zone 4 mapping meant "hours searching for them in the warren of streets," with some official listings already demolished or long in disuse. That Meryl detail is JoCI-only in this pass; use it only with that attribution.

**Zone 4 exact figures (2011 issue brief, unless noted):**

- 49 toilets surveyed and GPS-mapped, in a zone the brief describes as having a "considerable slum population." The Hindu, Sept. 15, 2011, identifies the zone as "Zone 4 (Ayanavaram)."
- Overlay, not a count: toilets were mapped against a 2005 TNSCB map of undeclared slums. "In Figure 1... each purple and blue dot corresponds to a slum, whereas each orange dot corresponds to a single public toilet. Take for instance, the northwest tip of Zone 4. One can see a cluster of slums (approximately 30,000 people), but not a single public toilet in the vicinity. Toilets also appear to be clustered in the south east corner of the zone... but are very few and far between in the northwest parts." This is the sharpest slum-adjacent number TC published: **zero public toilets near a northwest cluster of about 30,000 people.** It is a Zone 4 map reading, not a citywide total.
- 85 percent: "A majority, 85 percent, of the toilets we surveyed were being used by slum dwellers, and/or by workers in petty shops and construction sites." This is **who used the 49 toilets**, not how many toilets stood in slums. The same brief also says toilets were "away from slums, market areas, or bus stops." Do not convert 85% into a location statistic.
- Cleanliness: "Only 13 out of 49 toilets were documented as 'clean and hygienic' by the field staff."
- Men's only: 6 of 49 had facilities only for men; one of those had a women's section "being used exclusively by men."
- Bathing: "Approximately 50 per cent" had no bathing area (25 had none; 3 had a facility unusable for lack of doors or sewer blockage).
- Lights: 27 of 49 had "no lights within each latrine."
- Water: "approximately 80 percent did not have 24 hours water supply." Same figure in The Hindu, Sept. 15, 2011.
- Locked: 30 of 49 locked after 8 p.m.; 10 open 24 hours; 9 unknown. 2 of 49 locked permanently for sewer blockages; one women's section unused; one toilet built six months earlier still not opened.
- Caretaker: 18 of 49 had no caretaker or maintenance staff present at the visit.
- Fees: "barring 10 toilet locations, all others prescribe user fees," including "toilets in low-income settlements."

Sethuraman, Hindu op-ed, Apr. 27, 2012: more than half of the 49 were "barely used by residents, including women and children"; a 2005 TNSCB survey of "3.2 lakh residents of undeclared slums" found only 29% had individual toilets or pit latrines (the issue brief gives the same survey as "3.2 lakh slum households," 29% individual/pit, 48% public toilets, 22% other means including open defecation - "residents" vs "households" is a wording conflict; attribute and don't blend).

**Best lede numbers, if we cannot say "X of 600 were in slums":** the 572 vs 714 record-keeping split; the 49-toilet Zone 4 survey; 13 of 49 clean; 27 of 49 unlit; ~80% without 24-hour water; and the northwest overlay, about 30,000 people and not a single public toilet nearby.
