## 8. Rule 10 angle candidates

What the big outlets ran: ESPN, AP, FOX, Sportico, FOS and NFL.com ran the record price, closing, titles and cap table. CBS, PFN and PFT ran the Steelers line and the "500 years" line as gaffes or fan panic. Local TV ran the AI tutoring pledge as a community note. Sportico ran the gender and India-born firsts as two bullet points.

**A. "The Steelers were on TV when he landed." (PICK)**
- Spine (INTERPRETATION): the first loyalty test Seattle gave its new owner was about where a newly arrived student from India learned football. That was Pittsburgh, 1976-78, at Carnegie Mellon, in the Steel Curtain years. A Seattle fan base that lost Super Bowl XL to Pittsburgh read the old team as disloyalty.
- How it plays out: he answered with an all-caps X post and "Apologies in advance for my very transparent style". Five weeks later he did it again over "never say never". Meanwhile the team he's told to leave alone is 6-4.
- Why no one ran it: CBS explained Steelers fandom as a 1970s phenomenon. Nobody ran it as the immigrant's way into the sport, the way many desi dads adopted whatever team was winning when they landed. That thread is BRWNCLTR's.
- Backing FACTS: Atlanta line (Field Gulls, carried); Q9 (CBS; Seattle Sports); CBS on SB XL and the 2027 matchup; Q11-Q13 (Axios; PFT; KIRO); 6-4 record (ESPN).
- Risk: don't write "every desi dad" as fact. That generalization has to read as ours and be framed lightly, or cut.

**B. "The controlling owner wants to tutor Seattle's kids."**
- Spine (INTERPRETATION): the person who legally controls a $9.6B NFL team is a Stanford-trained education nonprofit founder. Sportico says she's the first woman to take the top spot straight out of a sale. She used her first podium to promise every Seattle kid a free AI tutor, while her husband and son split the football talk as an "office of the chairman".
- Backing FACTS: seahawks.com 3 Sep titles; Q2-Q4; KOMO "office of the chairman"; Sportico milestone; USA Today "front-facing".
- Risk: framing it as sidelining isn't supported, and she frames the work as her commitment. USA Today and Sportico touched the title-vs-face gap, so it's partly run.

**C. "Two desi bidders, one desi fan in the club seats."**
- Spine (INTERPRETATION): both finalists for the Super Bowl champions were led by men whose families built global fortunes from India: Khosla, and Aditya Mittal of ArcelorMittal (FOS). The closing list added a Seattle software founder who has sat in the same club seats since 2007 and calls himself "the 12th man".
- Backing FACTS: FOS 31 Aug & 3 Sep; GeekWire 3 Sep; seahawks.com co-owner list.
- Risk: FOS doesn't give Mittal's birthplace. No source gives Sunny Gupta's or Samir Kaul's heritage, and inferring it from surnames isn't allowed. **UNRESOLVED and blocking for this angle as framed.** It would need sourced heritage for each person named.

**Pick: A.** It has a person and a moment: Atlanta, 26 Aug, the line he volunteered. Its paper trail is his own words across three months (Atlanta, X on 28 Aug, Axios, X on 3 Oct). It's the diaspora read the sports press skipped. And it doesn't lean on the "first" framing that Khan complicates. B becomes the second movement (Neeru's title and pledge, so the piece isn't just Vinod). C stays out unless heritage is sourced.

- Suggested lede, first line (not article copy): "In 1976, a 21-year-old from India arrived in Pittsburgh just as the Steelers were winning Super Bowls, and fifty years later, standing in an Atlanta hotel as the man whose family was buying the Seattle Seahawks, Vinod Khosla admitted he still watches every Steelers game."
  - Checks: the age 21 is derived from ESPN's "born in India in 1955" (carried), which violates the origin-from-their-remarks rule. **Drop "21-year-old"** or replace it with his own "came to this country in '76". "From India" also comes from ESPN/AP reporter voice, not his words. Safer: "In 1976, Vinod Khosla came to Pittsburgh for grad school at Carnegie Mellon, just as the Steelers were winning Super Bowls..."
  - "Grad school" isn't in his post (it says "1976-78 period in Pittsburgh (CMU)"). Use "came to Pittsburgh, to Carnegie Mellon".
- Spine, one sentence: Vinod Khosla's Steelers confession, a relic of arriving in America at the height of the Steel Curtain, became the first loyalty test Seattle set for its new owners, and how he and the family have answered it since (all-caps posts, "never say never," a controlling owner pledging AI tutors, a 6-4 team they promised not to touch) is the real portrait of the Khoslas' first season.
