## Claim check

Labels: **FACT** (sourced), **INTERPRETATION** (a reading, not a fact), **UNRESOLVED**.

### Company

- **FACT.** On 11 May 2026 the company said it had announced $275 million in Series B financing at a $2 billion valuation and introduced the name Cowboy Space Corporation. It said it was founded in 2024 as Aetherflux. Source: company news post.
- **FACT.** The company said Index Ventures led the round, joined by new investors IVP, Blossom Capital, and SAIC, alongside existing investors Breakthrough Energy Ventures, Construct Capital, Andreessen Horowitz, NEA, Interlagos, and founder Baiju Bhatt. Source: company news post.
- **FACT.** The company said the rocket's upper stage and data-center payload are designed as a single vehicle, and that each upper stage is intended to become a one-megawatt data center once in orbit. Source: company news post.
- **FACT.** The company said it is collaborating with NVIDIA to deploy NVIDIA Space-1 Vera Rubin Modules in low Earth orbit. Source: company news post. This is an announced collaboration, not evidence that hardware is already on orbit.
- **FACT.** Bhatt told Payload the name was selected because "it captures the scale of ambition that we've got with the new set of objectives," and that it paints an optimistic picture of the company "blazing our own trail in space." Payload also reported the mission line "Powering humanity from the high frontier." Source: Feldscher, Payload, 11 May 2026.
- **FACT.** Payload reported a prior $50 million Series A, "a little over a year ago" from May 2026, as the company's first outside investment, aimed at a small-sat constellation to collect solar power and deliver it by infrared lasers. Index's Jan Hammer, 2 Apr 2025, wrote that Index was leading Aetherflux's $50 million Series A. TechCrunch the same day reported the $50 million round and a 2026 demonstration target.
- **FACT.** Payload reported that Cowboy Space planned to launch its first satellite "this year" (the article is dated 11 May 2026) to demo beaming power from space to Earth, and that Bhatt said the first rocket would launch no earlier than the end of 2028.
- **FACT.** Space.com (12 May 2026) reported the same rebrand, the Index-led $275 million round, the 1 MW upper-stage design, a 2024 founding, a $50 million Series A about a year earlier, a power-beaming satellite later in 2026, and a first rocket in 2028. Howell wrote that NVIDIA is providing an AI-focused chip platform; she also said engineers had come from NVIDIA. Those are her wordings. The company's own page is the cleaner source for the Vera Rubin module collaboration.
- **INTERPRETATION.** TechCrunch (Fernholz, 11 May 2026) called the $2 billion figure a post-money valuation. The company page says "at a $2 billion valuation" and does not use "post-money." Use the company's wording unless a retrieved primary release says post-money.
- **FACT (conflict to preserve).** Fernholz wrote that Bhatt "expects the first launch before the end of 2028." Payload wrote that Bhatt said the first rocket would launch "no earlier than the end of 2028." Those are not the same schedule. Space.com said the first rocket would launch in 2028. The draft must not collapse these into one date.
- **FACT.** Fernholz attributed to the company a satellite mass of 20,000 to 25,000 kilograms, 1 MW of power, and just under 800 onboard GPUs. The mass and GPU count are not on the company news page retrieved today. Attribute them to TechCrunch, or leave them out.
- **FACT.** TechCrunch, 9 Oct 2024, reported Aetherflux emerging from stealth and quoted the founding as "late last year," which from that dateline points at late 2023. The company's May 2026 page says founded in 2024. Public reveal is October 2024. Do not pick a secret incorporation date.
- **UNRESOLVED.** Full BusinessWire text of the 8 May 2026 URL. Not retrieved. A Jan Hammer Series B quote appeared only in a search snippet of that wire. Do not use it.
- **UNRESOLVED.** Whether the 2026 power-beaming flight is still on schedule as of 12 Oct 2026. No later primary update was checked.

### Bhatt: background, Robinhood, stake

- **FACT.** Robinhood's IR biography: he co-founded Robinhood with Vladimir Tenev in 2013 to democratize finance; co-CEO and co-President from 2013 to November 2020; Chief Creative Officer from 2021 to March 2024; director since founding; B.S. in Physics and M.S. in Mathematics from Stanford; two finance companies in New York before Robinhood.
- **FACT.** Robinhood DEF 14A filed 22 Apr 2026 lists him as a director nominee, age 41, director since 2013, "Founder and Chief Executive Officer, Aetherflux." The proxy predates the May rebrand, so the Aetherflux title there is expected.
- **FACT.** As of 8 Apr 2026 the proxy reports beneficial ownership of 3,579 Class A shares and 61,076,048 Class B shares (55.7% of Class B; 32.0% of voting power). The same footnotes say he has sole voting power but no investment power, and "does not have an economic interest," in 6,230,731 Class B shares of The Tenev 2017 Irrevocable Trust, 1,408,450 Class B shares of Butterfly Management LLC, and 1,574,375 Class B shares of Surfboard Management LLC. Beneficial ownership is not the same thing as "his stake."
- **INTERPRETATION.** Forbes (profile text updated 15 Sep 2026) says he "owns around 6%." That is in the neighborhood of an economic reading that strips the voting-only Class B shares out of the 8 Apr 2026 table and divides by Class A plus Class B shares outstanding that day. It is not a figure the proxy prints. Do not publish a single percentage without the voting-versus-economic distinction.
- **FACT (his words, Lifehacker, 2 Aug 2017).** He said he is an only child and the son of two immigrants; his parents moved to the United States when his father was accepted to a PhD program in theoretical physics at "University of Huntsville Alabama"; he grew up in Poquoson, Virginia; he went to Stanford to study physics, following his father; he met Vlad Tenev in college. Lifehacker said the interview was lightly edited.
- **FACT (his words, The Peel transcript, Turner Novak, 17 Jan 2025).** He said his father grew up in Bhavnagar in Gujarat in the 1970s and 80s; both parents come from big families in India; the family came to the U.S. so his father could study physics; his father later got a job "at NASA at Langley Air Force Base"; he studied physics at Stanford; he and Tenev met in the Stanford physics program. This is a published transcript, not a tape reviewed on this run.
- **UNRESOLVED.** The official name of the university. He said "University of Huntsville Alabama," not a catalog name. Do not silently rewrite it as the University of Alabama in Huntsville.
- **UNRESOLVED.** That his mother is from Gujarat. The Gujarat line in the transcript is his father's town. Lifehacker does not say Gujarati. Wikipedia's "son of Gujarati immigrants" cites Lifehacker, which does not contain that phrase.
- **FACT (attributed, not his sentence retrieved today).** TechCrunch, 9 Oct 2024, reported that his father worked for NASA at Langley Air Force Base. Index's Hammer, 2 Apr 2025, called him "the son of a NASA scientist." Use his Peel wording, or attribute these.
- **UNRESOLVED.** WSJ TikTok and the $100,000 "friendchecks" claim. A WSJ URL (Banerji, 21 June 2026) turned up in search with a headline about writing "Friendchecks." The page body was not retrieved (fetch timeout). The TikTok was not watched. Social reposts are not a WSJ text source. Do not state the dollar amount or what he said on the video.
