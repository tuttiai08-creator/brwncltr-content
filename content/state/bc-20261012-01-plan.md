## Proposed outline

Four beats. Not article prose. Each caveat once. The research packet holds the sourcing.

1. **Open.** No date in the lede. The guy who put free trading on your phone is building rockets, and he named the company Cowboy. Take in the first breath. Best Cowboy line up top if it fits without burying Robinhood: the name "captures the scale of ambition." One hedge, here only: the rocket has not flown. Don't open on Poquoson, a net-worth figure, or an identity label.

2. **Robinhood. This is the long section.** Founding: Stanford, Vlad Tenev, Celeris and Chronos as the two earlier companies TechCrunch named, the company in 2013, the predictions app that April, zero-commission announced that December, the trading app public on 12 March 2015. Free trades, then October 2019, when Schwab and E*TRADE went to $0 on the 7th and TD Ameritrade on the 3rd. Options contracts were not free. Confetti: Massachusetts alleged it on every trade in December 2020; the company, and the later consent order, describe firsts, and the order dates the stop at 31 March 2021. Say that split once. Roles: co-CEO until November 2020, then Tenev alone; CCO across 2021 to March 2024; do not pin the creative title to 20 November. Board the whole way. GameStop, accurately, once: 28 January 2021, buying restricted, Tenev testified on 18 February that the Securities president made the call. He was not CEO. He was not the witness. No public comment from him was found. IPO: 29 July 2021, priced at $38, closed at $34.82. Paper billionaire. Bloomberg says $2.5 billion, Forbes $2.4 billion. Name the outlet if a number appears. Family in a few lines inside the founding, then stop: Bhavnagar, Poquoson, the school he calls "University of Huntsville Alabama," NASA at Langley. No illness. No "as a South Asian."

3. **The second act.** Aetherflux, public October 2024, solar power down by infrared laser, $50 million Series A in April 2025. Then Cowboy, 11 May 2026: $275 million at a $2 billion valuation, Index leading, the company investor list, one vehicle, NVIDIA Space-1 Vera Rubin Modules, a 2026 satellite to beam power down. Rocket year, once: Payload says no earlier than the end of 2028; TechCrunch says before the end of 2028; Space.com says in 2028. Company says founded in 2024; TechCrunch that October said "late last year." Say that once.

4. **Landing.** Don't recap Robinhood. The picture is a $2 billion valuation waiting on one satellite. Does the 2026 power-beaming demo fly? End there. No moral. No investment advice. Don't rerun GameStop or the rocket-date split.
