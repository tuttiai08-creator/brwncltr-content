The founder who turned stock trading into an app is now building rockets and orbital data centers under the name Cowboy. ## Proposed outline

Chronological. No standalone list section. No prose yet.

1. **Intro.** May 2026, the new name, and one picture: a rocket whose upper stage is designed to remain in orbit as a one-megawatt data center. Say that nothing in that picture has flown. Do not open with childhood or with “Indian-American billionaire.”

2. **Hook.** Why the name is Cowboy. Bhatt’s Payload line that it captures the “scale of ambition,” and the mission sentence “Powering humanity from the high frontier.” The name is part of the pitch. Treat it as a choice, not a joke and not a brand case study.

3. **The round.** $275 million Series B, company wording “at a $2 billion valuation,” Index Ventures leading, and the investor list as the company published it (IVP, Blossom Capital, SAIC, Breakthrough Energy Ventures, Construct Capital, Andreessen Horowitz, NEA, Interlagos, and Bhatt). Note that Payload and TechCrunch printed shorter lists. Do not call the figure post-money unless the draft is quoting TechCrunch.

4. **The machine.** Stampede, in the company’s later public description, as a constellation of solar-powered orbital GPU data centers. The design choice: the upper stage and the data center are one vehicle. NVIDIA only as the announced plan to deploy Space-1 Vera Rubin Modules. Park the “about 800 GPUs” and the 20–25 tonne figures with TechCrunch, or cut them.

5. **The schedule, without smoothing it.** First satellite aimed at 2026 to demonstrate space-to-Earth power beaming (Payload; Space.com). Second mission, if used, only from a source that actually states early 2027. First rocket: quote the conflict and leave it visible — Payload, “no earlier than the end of 2028”; TechCrunch, “before the end of 2028”; Space.com, “in 2028.” Landing beat of this section: these are dates on a plan.

6. **How the company got there.** October 2024 public launch as Aetherflux, space solar power beamed down by infrared laser. April 2025, $50 million Series A led by Index and Interlagos, with Breakthrough Energy Ventures, Andreessen Horowitz, and NEA in the round TechCrunch described. The pivot: Bhatt’s account to TechCrunch that he could not get enough launch capacity, or the unit economics, by buying rides, so the company took on a rocket. Founding year: company says 2024; an October 2024 TechCrunch piece said “late last year.” Use both, do not adjudicate a filing date that was not retrieved.

7. **The person, in the order the work requires.** Stanford physics, and an M.S. in mathematics on the Robinhood biography. Met Vlad Tenev at Stanford; two earlier finance companies in New York; Robinhood in 2013; co-CEO until November 2020; chief creative officer until March 2024; still a director. Family only as far as it explains the turn toward space: immigrant parents, father from Bhavnagar, Gujarat, a physics PhD attempt, a NASA job at Langley, Poquoson. Stop before medical hardship. He is not the diaspora stand-in for this industry.

8. **What he still holds at Robinhood.** Director and co-founder, Class B voting power as reported in the April 2026 proxy (32% of voting power in that table), with an explicit note that beneficial ownership includes shares in which the footnotes say he has no economic interest. If a percentage of the company appears at all, it has to be the Forbes “around 6%” attributed as Forbes’s figure, not as the proxy’s. Prefer skipping a naked percentage.

9. **Landing.** The ambition is named, financed, and still on the ground. What a reader can watch: whether a 2026 power-beaming satellite actually launches, and which of the 2028 rocket sentences the company is willing to repeat. Close on the gap between a $2 billion valuation and hardware that has not flown. No investment advice. No prediction.
