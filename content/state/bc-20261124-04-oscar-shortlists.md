## The Story

Shortlists for the 99th Academy Awards are not out. Oscars.org sets the announcement for Tuesday, December 15, 2026, after preliminary voting from December 7 through December 11. On November 23, 2026 the Academy said 104 films are eligible for international feature, and its eligibility list names six titles tied to South Asia: *Gondhal* (India), *Elephants in the Fog* (Nepal), *Hanging by a Wire* (Pakistan), *Riverstone* (Sri Lanka), *Roid* (Bangladesh), and *Angh* (Toronto International Film Festival Platform Award).

## Why It's Interesting

Two of those titles already have a paper trail a culture desk can use, and they got there by different doors. The Print (Sept. 18, 2026) says the Film Federation jury picked Santosh Davakhar's Marathi thriller *Gondhal*, set over a single night of a gondhal ceremony in rural Maharashtra, as India's official submission. TIFF's July 21, 2026 programme note says *Angh*, by London-based writer-director Theja Rio from Nagaland, is his feature debut, an expansion of his short, shot in 16mm and set in the early 1960s near the Indo-Burma border. TIFF says he won the 2026 Platform Prize on Sept. 20. The Academy list enters *Angh* on that festival line, not on the India line.

The other four titles are on the Academy list with a country and nothing further opened this run. Craft shortlists (score, song, makeup, and the rest) are part of the same Dec. 15 announcement. No craft shortlist is published, and no South Asian craft contender was verified, so none is named here.

## BRWNCLTR Angle

For Rio, the origin is in TIFF's own note: a director from Nagaland, based in London, who turned his short into the feature that won Platform and is now on the Academy's eligibility list. That is the arc. *Gondhal* is the other Indian door, a Marathi night-long ceremony film chosen as the official submission. The piece, if either makes the Dec. 15 shortlist of 15, is which door held and what the film actually is. It is not a representation win, and it is not a claim that India has two films until we describe the two different lines on the Academy list.
