## Primary search intent / SEO topic

People who saw *Primetime*, or saw Ajon Singh's name on the Gotham list, and want to know who wrote it and how the movie got made.

## Suggested slug

ajon-singh-primetime

## Core angle

The group chat has Robert Pattinson's face and Chris Hansen's catchphrase. The movie's shape belongs to Ajon Singh: a script producers would not read, a five-day rewrite built off Pattinson's note that Hansen was an angel of punishment who should end as a weakened wizard, and an adaptation of Luke Dittrich's 2007 *Esquire* article that A24 credits to Singh alone. He is nominated for that screenplay at the Gothams. The piece is about those writing decisions.

## Why this story is worth reading

A recap can tell you the release date, the meme, and the opening weekend. It cannot tell you that he refused the young-journalist rise-and-fall, that he kept a cut airplane monologue in a Notes app until Lance Oppenheim threw it back at Pattinson, or that the character he cared about most was the struggling actor whose big break was playing a 13-year-old. That is the difference between the Pattinson story and the writer's story. US desks already did the first one.

## Proposed outline

No article prose. Section titles are the planned H2s. Each beat is a sourced move, not a paragraph.

1. **Hook — the bathroom, then five days.** Open on Singh at Pattinson's house in Los Angeles, leaving the room to type notes, then five days alone on the Hansen rewrite. One quote, from Singh to The Nod: Pattinson called the character "this angel of punishment" and, by the third act, "a weakened wizard." Land on Pattinson saying he could do it. No date. No awards logistics. No "not a biopic" sentence.

2. **The nut.** One beat on why this week: his name is on the Gotham Best Adapted Screenplay list, ceremony Monday, Nov. 30, at Cipriani Wall Street, also on YouTube. The take, in a sentence: the meme is the performance; the nomination is the pages. Attribute the nomination to the Gotham release.

3. **He wanted the movie to start on the cusp.** Producers asked for a page-one rise-and-fall, a young journalist coming up. He held the *Network* shape: loud, then a crash into silence. One quote from The Nod. Do not add a section whose job is to say what the movie is not.

4. **The article under the script.** Luke Dittrich, *Esquire*, Sept. 1, 2007, "Tonight on Dateline This Man Will Die." A24's for-your-consideration page: Best Adapted Screenplay, Ajon Singh, "Based on the article by Luke Dittrich." Dittrich told *The Hollywood Reporter* the script is Singh's, that he never read it, and that he had no creative input. What Singh told *The Cold Magazine* he took from the piece: the 20-something actor whose break was playing a child, and a producer he turned into a composite. Bill Conradt named once, as the person the *Esquire* story is about, no method and no photos. Do not repeat Elle India's "co-wrote with Luke Dittrich."

5. **The kid and the card.** Insider section. Dan's audition stayed in every draft. Singh told The Nod he saw himself in that kid. Test audiences found the original ending too bleak. After a dinner, Skyler Gisondo's suggestion — a SAG-AFTRA card — replaced a dead dream with the question "Are you an actor?" One quote. This is the section a meme recap does not have.

6. **What he was watching.** One scene, not a résumé. His words to The Nod: a family of immigrants, Oprah, *Dateline*, *To Catch a Predator* sneak-watched because he thought it was funny, understood later. To *The Cold Magazine*: he was around 10. The Bollywood beat is craft, one quote: *Sholay*, *Bobby*, *Don*, *Devdas*, *Rang De Basanti*, *Dhoom*, and dialogue he hears as rhythm, including teaching himself "Dhoom Machale." No Uber-to-CAA-to-*Painkiller* tour. No "as a South Asian" frame. The Bronx / Iowa / Phoenix / Los Angeles list stays out of the copy until a human has listened to the podcast (see verification).

7. **The next room, then the landing.** One beat only on Spider-Punk: Daniel Kaluuya read the *Primetime* script, called, and asked Singh to write it with him (Singh to THR); Deadline and Variety, Aug. 5, 2025, had it in early development at Sony, co-written with Kaluuya; Kaluuya told Deadline on Dec. 7, 2025 that the first draft was in its "finishing stages," plot "TBD," no date. Then land on the screenplay nomination, or on the last question in the movie. No pun. Do not close on the "handful of Indian and Indian-American people" line. That quote can sit in the packet; it is not the ending.
