## Full draft

# Susha Didn't Know It Was Grammy Nomination Day. The Song Her Mother Taught Her Was on the Ballot.

*Devan Ekambaram set a Subramania Bharati poem around Susha's voice and put it out as a one-song single. The Recording Academy put it next to Shakira and Rosalía.*

Susha was in the middle of a studio shoot on Nov. 16, with her phone switched off. When she turned it back on, Hindustan Times reported, hundreds of messages were waiting. "It wasn't in my consciousness that yesterday was the announcement of the nominations," she told Billboard India the next day. "For me, it was just any other day."

Devan Ekambaram was praying at a temple in Chennai when the pings started. "I got 15 congratulations at once," he told Hindustan Times. "I did puja-archana and let the news sync in."

The news was "Varuvaaiy," their setting of a Tamil devotional poem by Subramania Bharati, nominated for Best Global Music Performance at the 69th Grammy Awards. The Academy's nominee line reads "Devan Ekambaram, Susha," and the category is crowded with famous names. Shakira and Burna Boy are in it for "Dai Dai," Angélique Kidjo, Pharrell Williams and Quavo for "Bando [Orchestral]," and Rosalía and Dougie F for "Porcelana," alongside KerreKe, Mireya Ramos and Alex E. Chávez for "Una Flor En Cada Estrella." Winners will be announced Feb. 7, 2027.

"Varuvaaiy" came out Aug. 12 as a single on Apple Music, three minutes and 26 seconds long, with no record company named on it.

### A song from her mother

The idea was Devan's: he was working on a full album of Bharati's songs in June, The Hindu reported, and asked Susha to sing on it. She wanted to skip the famous ones. "There are many popular songs of his," she told Billboard India. "But when Devan said he wanted to do a Bharathiar song, I wanted to find something that wasn't so obvious. This was a song my mother taught me years ago."

Her mother, the Carnatic singer Lakshmi Rangarajan, had first heard the song at a concert and liked it enough to learn it herself, Susha told The Hindu. The poem is addressed to Krishna. Its title, Devan told Hindustan Times, means "Krishna, won't you come to me!" Its refrain keeps landing on *Kanna*, a word of affection for Krishna.

### Her voice first

Devan built the track backward. "I recorded her voice and then made everything around her singing and added my vocals and arrangements," he told Hindustan Times. Even the raga was partly her call, as he tells it. He wanted Raag Bardiya, he said, and she suggested Des; Indian outlets and his own campaign page call it Desh.

Prakash Sontakke plays slide guitar on the record, and Anil Srinivasan plays piano. Neither is on the nominee line. Srinivasan told The Hindu he was happy to see Bharati "on centrestage here — the grandest global one at that."

The moment Devan keeps coming back to is the ending. "At the end, she keeps repeating 'Kanna' with different improvisations," he told Billboard India. "Till today, I cry every time I hear that section."

### Two careers, one song

Devan has been around Tamil film music for a long time. He calls himself a composer who kept getting asked to sing, and took the work. "Oh Maria" in *Kadhalar Dhinam* was one of those songs, and he has worked with A.R. Rahman, Harris Jayaraj and Ilaiyaraaja, he told The Hindu. He last composed for a film in 2010, *Bale Pandiya*. He has entered the Grammys before, spreading three or four songs across categories, until a Grammy winner told him not to be a jack of all trades, he told Billboard India. This year he put everything behind one song. "I never, in a million years, thought that out of 526 submissions, this would somehow make the top five," he said.

Susha has been singing Carnatic music since she was five, learning from her mother and grandmother, she told Hindustan Times, and performing professionally since she was about 13. The Hindu reports she later earned a master's in jazz performance and composition at the University of Toronto. On Aug. 7 she released *Love Language*, her first album of original music, under her own name, in English and Tamil. "Varuvaaiy" came out five days later under his.

"Devan is not from the Carnatic scene," she told Hindustan Times, "but this collaboration came out of the blue so we both are very excited."

### The first, and the only

Devan's Instagram caption, reprinted across Indian outlets, called "Varuvaaiy" "the very 1st Tamil Song to ever be nominated." Many headlines repeated it. The Hindu hedged with "reportedly," and the Academy's own pages don't make the claim. Several outlets also called it India's only nomination this year. The Academy's list doesn't sort nominees by country, and Gorillaz's *The Mountain*, nominated for Best Alternative Music Album, features Anoushka Shankar, Asha Bhosle and other Indian musicians, though none of them is on its nominee line.

### Next

There's no music video yet; The Hindu said the team is still thinking about one. The Bharati album Devan was making in June hasn't been announced. He has a film score coming too, for the Tamil-Telugu bilingual *Pattarai*. Final-round Grammy voting runs Dec. 10 to Jan. 7.

"For now, all my focus is on taking 'Varuvaaiy' to the next level," he told The Hindu.
