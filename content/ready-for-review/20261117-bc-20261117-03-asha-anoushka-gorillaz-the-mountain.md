## Full draft

# Asha Bhosle and Anoushka Shankar Are All Over Gorillaz's Grammy-Nominated Album

*Bhosle's boatman song on* The Mountain *was one of her final recordings. Shankar's sitar runs through half the record. The nomination says Gorillaz.*

Damon Albarn sat cross-legged on the floor in Mumbai, playing R.D. Burman's harmonium, while Asha Bhosle sang. She was 91, and he had admired her from afar for years. "Her voice was my gateway to the marvellous world of Bollywood," he wrote in April, two days after she died. "The memory of sitting cross-legged playing R. D. Burman's harmonium while she sang will stay with me forever."

That voice is on "The Shadowy Light," the 12th track of *The Mountain*, the ninth Gorillaz album. Anoushka Shankar's sitar is on seven others. On Nov. 16, the Recording Academy nominated the album for Best Alternative Music Album at the 2027 Grammys, alongside records from Phoebe Bridgers, Charli XCX, Geese, Alabama Shakes and ROSALÍA. The nominee listed on the Academy's page is Gorillaz. Neither woman's name appears on that line.

Most of the coverage has given them a sentence at most. Pitchfork's write-up of the category didn't mention either of them, and the Indian Express folded them into a single line in a story about another nominee. They were much more than guests on the record.

### An album about death

The record started with a family emergency. At the end of 2022, Jamie Hewlett flew to Jaipur after his mother-in-law had a stroke and spent two months there. From there, he told Albarn they had to make a Gorillaz project in India. The two went together in May 2023, and after they got back to London, both of their fathers died, ten days apart. Albarn took his father's ashes to Varanasi and scattered them in the Ganges.

"If we can make an album about death that makes people feel less afraid of the concept of it, wouldn't that be an amazing gift?" Hewlett said he asked Albarn, according to Rolling Stone India.

### The boatman

Bhosle almost said no. "I was hesitant to work on this album of Gorillaz," she told Rolling Stone India, "but when I heard the music track and the lyrics, it triggered something deep inside me." The Hindi lyric is by Kausar Munir, who shares the writing credit on "The Shadowy Light" with Albarn and Gruff Rhys, who also sings on it. Ajay Prasanna's bansuri and the sarods of Amaan Ali Bangash and Ayaan Ali Bangash are on the track too.

The song asks a boatman to carry the singer across deep water. In February, days before the album came out, Bhosle explained to Vogue India what that crossing meant to her.

"I'm telling the boatman to ferry me across the river, which is my life's journey: my birth, my relationships, my dedication to music, my achievements, my duties as a mother, daughter, sister, wife and Indian," she said. "The boatman is a metaphor for my music, which has guided me across this river of life."

### The sitar in London

Shankar came in after the India trips were done. By the time Gorillaz reached out, she told The Nod, "they'd already had their big India trip that made them want to make an album about their experiences." They wanted her to play sitar, and she recorded with Albarn in London.

It grew from there. "They were very open about which songs to put sitar on, and after one or two, they must have liked my style, so we kept doing more," she told The Hollywood Reporter India. She ended up billed on six tracks, including the title song, "Orange County" and "The Sad God," and she's a writer on five of them. The credits give her a seventh sitar part, on "The Moon Cave." On one track with "Prince" in the title, she suggested layering fast strumming into what she called "a cocoon for the voice."

For Albarn, the sessions reached back to his own childhood. His father loved Indian classical music and played him Ravi Shankar records as a baby. "I probably listened to Ravi Shankar before I listened to The Beatles when I was a kid," Albarn told Rolling Stone UK. On CBC's *Q*, he called improvising with Ravi's daughter "a really extraordinary moment."

At the album's live unveiling, Shankar told The Nod, the band asked her to just stay onstage, because she was on so many tracks. In February she played "Orange County" with Gorillaz and Kara Jackson on *The Graham Norton Show*. A few weeks later, for the band's *Saturday Night Live* debut, she was back, playing "The Moon Cave" with Black Thought and Asha Puthli, a different Asha from the one on "The Shadowy Light."

### April

*The Mountain* came out on Feb. 27. Six weeks later, on April 12, Bhosle died in Mumbai at 92, her son confirmed, the BBC reported. Her career ran more than eight decades, through the long partnership with R.D. Burman, whom she married in 1980, and the 1997 Cornershop hit named after her. The BBC called "The Shadowy Light" one of her final recordings.

"Asha, you had the voice of an angel," Albarn wrote. "We love you."

### The name on the line

Neither woman is new to the Grammys. Bhosle was nominated twice: in 1997 with Ali Akbar Khan, and in 2006 with the Kronos Quartet for an album of Burman's songs. Shankar has 14 nominations, the latest two at the 2026 ceremony. Neither has won.

The 2027 Grammys air Feb. 7. If *The Mountain* wins, the trophy goes to Gorillaz. The record itself credits both women, track by track, and Bhosle had already said what the crossing meant to her.

"When I get to the other side, my journey will be complete and I will attain moksha," she told Vogue India. "If you listen carefully, you will be able to discern thousands of sounds floating around us. I shall become one of them."
