## Full draft

# Asha Bhosle and Anoushka Shankar Helped Make Gorillaz's Grammy-Nominated Album

*Bhosle's boatman song on* The Mountain *was one of her final recordings. Neither name is on the nomination.*

Damon Albarn sat cross-legged in Mumbai, playing R.D. Burman's harmonium, while Asha Bhosle sang. She was 91, and he had admired her from afar for years. When he wrote about that day in April, two days after she died, he said the memory would stay with him forever.

Her voice is on "The Shadowy Light," and Anoushka Shankar's sitar runs through seven more songs on *The Mountain*, which the Recording Academy nominated for Best Alternative Music Album on Nov. 16.

### An album about death

Gorillaz made the record after Albarn and Jamie Hewlett lost their fathers, ten days apart, and Albarn scattered his father's ashes in the Ganges at Varanasi.

Hewlett put the idea to Albarn as a question: "If we can make an album about death that makes people feel less afraid of the concept of it, wouldn't that be an amazing gift?"

### The boatman

Kausar Munir wrote the Hindi lyric, and Bhosle sings it over bansuri and two sarods, with Gruff Rhys beside her.

"I was hesitant to work on this album of Gorillaz, but when I heard the music track and the lyrics, it triggered something deep inside me," Bhosle told Rolling Stone India.

The song asks a boatman to carry the singer across deep water. In February, days before the album came out, Bhosle told Vogue India that the river was her life's journey: her birth, her relationships, her music, her duties as a mother, daughter, sister, wife and Indian.

"The boatman is a metaphor for my music, which has guided me across this river of life," she said.

### The sitar in London

Shankar had never worked with Gorillaz when they reached out wanting sitar, and she ended up recording with Albarn in London, including one free-flowing, exploratory session. By the time they called, she said, "they'd already had their big India trip that made them want to make an album about their experiences."

She has a writing credit on five songs. On "The Sweet Prince," she suggested fast strumming that would create what she called "a cocoon for the voice." The title song might sound very Indian, she told Rolling Stone India, but it's not necessarily based on a raga.

"The music is very inherently them," she said. "The melodies are being played with inflections and improvisations that are a bit more Indian-influenced in nature, because of who's playing it in those moments."

For Albarn, the sessions reached back to his own childhood. His father loved Indian classical music and played Ravi Shankar records to him when he was a baby. "I probably listened to Ravi Shankar before I listened to The Beatles when I was a kid," he told Rolling Stone UK.

When the band unveiled the album live, they asked her to stay onstage because she was on so many tracks. In February she turned up again, playing "Orange County" with Kara Jackson on *The Graham Norton Show*, and weeks later she was back for the band's *Saturday Night Live* debut, playing "The Moon Cave" with Black Thought and Asha Puthli.

### April

*The Mountain* came out on Feb. 27, and about six weeks later, on April 12, Bhosle died in Mumbai at 92. Her career ran more than eight decades, through the long partnership with R.D. Burman, whom she married in 1980, and the 1997 Cornershop hit named after her. The BBC called "The Shadowy Light" one of her final recordings.

Albarn ended his tribute with a line to her directly: "Asha, you had the voice of an angel, we love you."

### The other side

Bhosle has two Grammy nominations and Shankar has 14, and neither has won.

Bhosle told Vogue India that when she reached the other side of the river, her journey would be complete and she would attain moksha.

"If you listen carefully, you will be able to discern thousands of sounds floating around us. I shall become one of them."
