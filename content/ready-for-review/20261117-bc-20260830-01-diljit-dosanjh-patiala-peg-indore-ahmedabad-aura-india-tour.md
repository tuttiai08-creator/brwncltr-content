## Full draft

In Indore on Sunday night, Diljit Dosanjh sang "Patiala Peg," one of four songs he'd been told to drop. The police had cleared the concert at the last minute, on the condition that all four stayed off the setlist, and before the night was over he'd also brought young fans up on stage with him.

The notice came from Indore's district child protection officer, Rajneesh Sinha. On November 6 he wrote to the venue asking it to keep out "Patiala Peg," "5 Taara," "Case" and "Jatt Uthe Fire Karda," acting on a complaint from a Chandigarh professor, Panditrao Dharenavar. The letter cited a 2019 Punjab and Haryana High Court order directed at the police chiefs of Punjab, Haryana and Chandigarh. Indore is in Madhya Pradesh. After the concert, Sinha told PTI he was still waiting to read the order himself: "I have asked the complainant to send me a copy of the order. Only after studying it will it be clear whether it prohibits the performance of any specific song by a particular singer at events such as live concerts."

Diljit had already answered from the stage. "You can't sing this, you can't sing that. You can't do this, you can't do that," he told the crowd, in PTI's translation. Then he reached for Rahat Indori and recited the couplet that ends, in PTI's text, "kisi ke baap ka Hindostan thodi hai". Later he put a fan video of "Patiala Peg" on his Instagram story.

Britain heard three of those songs very differently. Six days after Wembley, at a sold-out arena in Birmingham, "5 Taara" was the second song of the night, "Case" was the third and "Patiala Peg" came in at eleven, all before the intermission. That's three of the four songs Indore tried to take away. At home the same catalog arrives with paperwork, a letter in one city and a police condition in another, while a former Supreme Court judge and an advocate, in India Today, disagree over who's allowed to decide. The diaspora show and the home show have split apart, and Diljit is spending the end of the year walking between them.

### What Britain heard

Wembley was the peak. The stadium billed him as the first Punjabi artist to headline it, and on September 12 he opened with a twist on his old catchphrase, "Punjabi Aa Gaye Wembley Oye!" He sang Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan's "Sanson Ki Mala" with phone lights up, and he closed with "Main Hoon Punjab" under fireworks. A rule on record that came from his side was about the crowd: the tour asked Wembley to keep flags, posters and signs out of the building.

Before the show he told the BBC what the booking meant to him. "It may not mean as much for someone like Harry Styles, as much as it means for us," he said. "He has performed on such stages, but for us, it's very special." Styles had spent the same summer playing a twelve-night run at Wembley, and Diljit got one night, the one that closed the stadium's season.

### The film went first

India's fight with him didn't start in Indore. In July his film *Satluj*, the Jaswant Singh Khalra story that was once titled *Punjab '95*, premiered on ZEE5 and was pulled from the platform in India two days later. By July 11 it was gone from ZEE5 everywhere, and ZEE5 Global said it could not offer the film to its viewers. Diljit had seen the takedown coming. In a live video, in the BBC's translation, he said he'd expected a ban once government offices opened on Monday, "but I didn't know it would happen as early as Sunday evening."

The India tour nearly lost its opener too. Delhi Police turned down permission for the show on October 21, citing security concerns and threat inputs, then reversed itself, and on October 30 he walked out at Jawaharlal Nehru Stadium in front of more than 70,000 people, Billboard India reported. From that stage he answered the people who'd mocked *Satluj*. "Don't teach Punjabis how to fight battles," he told them.

### Hyderabad, 2024

Dharenavar has been chasing three of these songs since at least 2024. In November 2024, after he submitted video, the Telangana government served a notice on Diljit's Hyderabad show, and Diljit worked around it on stage: "5 taara hotel ch" instead of "5 taara theke utte", and "daaru" became "Coke" in "Lemonade". A few days later, at a 2024 show in Ahmedabad, he left the alcohol songs out entirely and said that if every state went dry like Gujarat, he'd never sing about liquor again. In Indore this month he sang "Patiala Peg" anyway.

### Saturday in Ahmedabad

Ahmedabad is next, and the paperwork got there first. On November 6 the city's district child protection officer, Tanvika Parmar, wrote to the police commissioner asking him to stop the same kind of songs, keep children off the stage and bar bandoliers as depicted in the show's promotional material. Her letter says the songs do harm "even with altered or twisted lyrics," which shuts the door he walked through in Hyderabad. Not everyone thinks a child protection officer gets to make that call. Former Supreme Court judge Madan B Lokur told India Today that the officer had no authority to issue such directions.

Diljit announced this show himself, from the Wembley stage in September. He told London that the biggest stadium is in India: "India mein stadium ho, aur Punjabi udhar na ho, yeh toh phir ho nahi sakta." The show is Saturday, November 21, at Narendra Modi Stadium.
