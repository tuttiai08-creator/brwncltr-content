## Full draft

Vinod Khosla got to Pittsburgh in 1976, in the middle of the Steelers' run of four Super Bowls in six years. Fifty years later, in Atlanta, with his family about to own the Seattle Seahawks, he admitted he still watches every Steelers game.

Ten games into their first season, the Khoslas have had to answer to Seattle three times: for the Steelers, for the 49ers and for a rumor that the team might leave.

"I happened to come to this country in '76 when the Steelers were winning the Super Bowl," Khosla told the Seattle Times' Bob Condotta, after NFL owners signed off on his family's purchase. "So actually, I was originally, and still am, a big Steelers fan, also." In Seattle, the Steelers are still the team that beat the Seahawks in their first Super Bowl, in 2006.

### Seattle's first test

The clip didn't stay in Atlanta. Barstool Sports posted it as a lesson in what not to do at your first press conference as owner, and one fan summed it up: "We got sold to a Steelers fan with a 49ers fan son, we're cooked." Two days after the vote, Khosla answered on X with a post that opened by blaming reporters and then got to the point: "What's wrong with admitting my 1976-78 period in Pittsburgh (CMU) got me excited about the NFL?"

The rest of that post ran on capital letters and exclamation points, swearing that as owner he'd always want Seattle to win, and he signed off by apologizing in advance for his style. The same day he posted praise for the logo and the 12s, spelled "12's." A fan pointed out there's no apostrophe, and Khosla wrote back one word: "Learning!"

### The family takes over

The sale closed on September 3 with Neeru Khosla as controlling owner, Vinod as chair and their son Neal as vice chair. Sportico has reported that Neeru is the first woman to take the top ownership spot at an NFL team straight out of a sale.

Six days later the Khoslas sat down at Lumen Field for their introductory press conference, and Vinod's plan for a team that had just won the Super Bowl was short. "How lucky is that? How often do you get to buy a team that just won the Super Bowl?" he said. "So the best thing we can do is not mess with a lot." The one change they announced for fans was the return of the white throwback uniforms in 2027.

Neeru used her time at the microphone to talk about kids. "I am here to bring AI Tutoring so that every kid in Seattle can get a tutor, personal tutor," she said. "So it will be free. I'm serious about that."

### The 49ers problem

Pittsburgh isn't the only old team in the family's life. Vinod told Axios that he and his son have probably gone to half of all 49ers home games together over the past 28 years, and the family went into the Seattle deal holding a 3.1% stake in San Francisco. Neal had already seen Lumen Field as a 49ers minority owner, at Seattle's 30-point playoff win over the 49ers in January. "We walked away going, 'Oh my God. Like, this is built different,'" Neal said at the press conference.

Asked in the Axios interview about cheering a 49ers touchdown, Vinod said, "No, I'm not confused." Less than two weeks later the 49ers came to Lumen Field and won 32-29.

### Five hundred years

The same interview set off Seattle's relocation scare. "I will never say never. Will this team be in Seattle 500 years from now?" Vinod said, before adding that he had no current plans to change anything about the team, Lumen Field included. "Never say never" traveled a lot faster than the rest, and two days later Vinod was back on X.

This time he kept it short. "To all @Seahawks fans and @12s, we have no plans to move the team, have never discussed it, haven't even thought about it. End of story," he wrote.

Seattle is 6-4 and has won two straight. The Steelers are on next year's schedule.
