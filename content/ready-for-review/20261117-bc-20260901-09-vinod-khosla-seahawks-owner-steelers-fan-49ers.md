## Full draft

Ten days after the Seattle Seahawks beat the New England Patriots 29–13 in Super Bowl LX, Paul Allen’s estate announced it was selling the team. Fans had just lined the streets for a championship parade. By the time the Patriots came back for the season opener in September and Seattle unveiled its second Super Bowl banner, the Khosla family owned the Seahawks.

At the NFL owners meeting in Atlanta in August, Vinod Khosla told reporters he came to this country in ’76 and still watches all the Steelers games. He landed in Pittsburgh in the middle of the Steelers’ run of four Super Bowl wins in six seasons. In Seattle, Pittsburgh is still the team that beat the Seahawks in their first Super Bowl. One fan who saw the clip had already sized up the family: “We got sold to a Steelers fan with a 49ers fan son, we’re cooked.”

That same week, Khosla praised the Seahawks logo and the “12’s” on X. A fan pointed out that the 12s don’t take an apostrophe. Khosla replied with one word: “Learning!” The next day he wrote about what those years in Pittsburgh meant to him and promised, in capital letters, that he’d always want Seattle to win.

Khosla co-founded Sun Microsystems in 1982, spent 18 years at Kleiner Perkins, and then started Khosla Ventures, which put seed money into DoorDash in 2013. The Khoslas’ group bought the Seahawks for $9.612 billion, an NFL record.

The NFL had seen a South Asian owner take a similar path. Shahid Khan came to the U.S. from Pakistan at 16 to study engineering at the University of Illinois, got into the NFL watching games with his fraternity brothers, and made his money in auto parts, buying his former employer, Flex-N-Gate, and turning it into a global supplier before NFL owners approved his purchase of the Jacksonville Jaguars in 2011.

The Seahawks’ controlling owner is Neeru Khosla, who co-founded CK-12, a nonprofit that provides free educational materials, and now also leads the Seahawks Charitable Foundation. Vinod is chair, and their son Neal is vice chair. Neeru said in Atlanta that it was Neal who pushed the family to make the bid. He and his dad had been going to football games together for years. Vinod told Axios they’d probably made it to half of the 49ers’ home games over a span of 28 years. The family even went into the Seattle deal with a minority stake in San Francisco.

In that same interview, Axios asked whether Vinod would commit to keeping the Seahawks in Seattle long-term. “I will never say never. Will this team be in Seattle 500 years from now?” he said. He added that he had no current plans to change anything about the team, including Lumen Field. Two days later, Khosla wrote on X that there were no plans to move the Seahawks. “End of story,” he said.

Seattle had come close enough to losing the Seahawks once that the moving vans showed up. That was in 1996, when then-owner Ken Behring started moving the team to Southern California. Paul Allen bought the Seahawks the following year and kept them in Seattle. This February, the team Allen kept in town brought the Lombardi Trophy home, and fans lined the streets to see it.
