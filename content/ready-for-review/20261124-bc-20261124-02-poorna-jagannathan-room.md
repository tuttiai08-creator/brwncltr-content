## Full draft

# Poorna Jagannathan Keeps Getting Handed the Room

*A crime-boss auntie, then the head of an FBI task force, and a senator next if Hulu picks up the show.*

In the third episode of Apple TV's *Nocturne*, a girl is still missing, the task force has lost contact with its undercover agent, and the ex-cop working the case gets around that by having someone break into the off-site servers that store the security footage from the facility where the serial killer is locked up. Poorna Jagannathan plays Alexandra Quinn, the FBI special agent in charge, and Quinn is furious, because nothing from that footage will ever be admissible in court. She also knows the alternative is leaving her agent blind.

That's the job Jagannathan keeps getting cast in lately: the person everyone else answers to, played by a woman who still sounds a little surprised to be there. "I'm turning 54," she told Variety India in September. "So there's this moment where I am like, 'What am I doing here, or in a superhero universe like *Lanterns*?'"

Her co-star Liev Schreiber, who plays a former detective on the show, told The Indian Express that her approach to Quinn was straightforward and cutting. "It was great to see her just dominate the room," he said. "And she often did that with humour." Her own pitch for the character is shorter: the locals botched the case, she told the LA Times, "so they decided to bring in the big guns."

Lucky came first, on Hulu's *Deli Boys*. Naveeda "Lucky" is the family confidant who has to tell two pampered Pakistani American brothers, after their father dies, that behind the deli counter, hidden in the achar jars, sits a multimillion-dollar drug ring. The creator, Abdullah Saeed, originally wrote Lucky as a man and switched her after a Geena Davis talk pushed writers to turn male characters into women, and what came out was a woman in a leather jumpsuit, stilettos and three guns who does business with Italian, Peruvian, Vietnamese and Indian gangsters, all of them men. "She's very comfortable and intimidating," Jagannathan told Vanity Fair. Told that Lucky was a cool auntie, she pushed back: "She's not a cool auntie! She's a murderous auntie!"

The role won her a Gotham Award, and it landed where her life already was. She started acting late, got *Never Have I Ever* at 48 and Lucky at 52, and to her that order is the point. "There is an absolute freedom you get when you're older," she said. "I remember in my 20s, when I'd say 'no,' I'd feel guilty. Now, when I say 'yes,' I regret it immediately."

Hulu canceled *Deli Boys* in August after two seasons, and that same summer she turned out to be the villain on HBO's *Lanterns*. Talking to the AV Club about both parts, she drew a line most actors don't bother to draw: "There's a difference between being strong and powerful. With Lucky and Zoe, everything revolves around them."

*Nocturne* doesn't always hold up its end. In the first episode, Schreiber's Jonah walks into the task force briefing she's running and pretty much takes it over, and five episodes in she's still reluctantly putting up with his unannounced missions. Decider's review counted Quinn among the show's underwritten characters and wished *Nocturne* put her in the spotlight even more. She has the title and not nearly enough of the script.

The next one might give her more. In October, Hulu cast her as a series regular in *Young Americans*, a pilot executive produced by *The Handmaid's Tale* creator Bruce Miller. She plays Maya Washburn, the President's former close friend and ally, now a controversial senator rallying a disillusioned electorate while her son falls in love with his daughter. The show hasn't been ordered to series yet, and in the meantime she's filming season two of Dan Levy's *Big Mistakes* for Netflix and an indie, *Twenty-Seven*, with Anthony Hopkins.

In episode five, Quinn is back on the phone with her ex-husband, still fighting over their kid, and the job that put her in charge is the same one he's using to argue for full custody. Episode six is out Nov. 27.
