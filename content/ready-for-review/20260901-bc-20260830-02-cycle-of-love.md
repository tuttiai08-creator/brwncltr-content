## Full draft

In 1977, P.K. Mahanandia got on a bicycle in Delhi and set out to find Lotta, the Swedish woman who had once asked him to draw her portrait.

That is the story *The Cycle of Love* puts on the poster, and Orlando von Einsiedel’s documentary has already had its theatrical life: it opened in New York on 28 August and at Laemmle’s Royal in West Los Angeles on 4 September, ran at the Royal through 17 September, and, Deadline reported, returned for a one-day “date night” on 1 November, then rental and purchase from 2 November on Amazon Prime Video, Apple TV, and YouTube. Priyanka Chopra Jonas is an executive producer.

The poster says 6,000 miles by bike. “I didn't just travel by bicycle. I got rides with trucks,” he told National Geographic. The movie’s logline and the older interviews also do not match on whether he was 23 or 26, or whether the distance was 6,000 miles or 3,600 kilometers, and the older journalism already included trains, trucks, and lifts.

As a schoolboy in a village in Orissa, he often faced discrimination from upper-caste students because he was Dalit. “I was below the dogs and cows,” he told CNN, describing being forced to sit outside the schoolroom. “The caste system in India is organized racism,” he told National Geographic.

Von Einsiedel told Variety: “For me, the most powerful part of P.K.’s story when I interviewed him was that he carried a lot of trauma from growing up under the caste system,” and that the journey “allowed him to learn to love himself,” which sits next to Variance’s Dylan Marchetti calling the film “a beautiful romantic universal story” and the director asking why documentaries can’t be “about happiness and joy.”

Lotta von Schedvin is Swedish; the 2016 interviews call her Charlotte. They met in 1975 in Delhi’s Connaught Place, when she asked him to draw her portrait. The film’s logline still says “untouchable,” and BBC and CNN say Dalit.

He did not have enough money to buy a plane ticket, so he sold everything he owned, bought a bicycle, and left Delhi in January 1977, reaching Europe in May and taking a train to Gothenburg, and CNN has him taking lifts.

In October the Critics Choice Association nominated von Einsiedel for Best Director and Franklin Dow for Best Cinematography.

“I was cycling for love, but never loved cycling,” he told the BBC. “It's simple.”
