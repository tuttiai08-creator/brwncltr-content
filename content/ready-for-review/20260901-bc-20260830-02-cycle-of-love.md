## Full draft

In 1977, P.K. Mahanandia got on a bicycle in Delhi to go find Lotta von Schedvin, the Swedish woman who had asked him to draw her portrait in Connaught Place a year earlier.

That's the story on the poster for Orlando von Einsiedel's documentary *The Cycle of Love*, which you can now rent on Amazon Prime Video, Apple TV and YouTube. But in the interviews he gave the BBC, CNN and National Geographic years before the film, he keeps coming back to caste.

He grew up Dalit in a village in Orissa, and his school made him sit outside the classroom. "I was below the dogs and cows," he told CNN in 2016. A year later he put it more bluntly: "The caste system in India is organized racism."

Even von Einsiedel says caste is the core: "The most powerful part of P.K.'s story when I interviewed him was that he carried a lot of trauma from growing up under the caste system," he told Variety. His U.S. distributor, Variance, still sells it as "a beautiful romantic universal story."

What "universal" leaves out is already in his interviews: a Dalit schoolboy made to sit outside, a caste system he called organized racism, and a ride he said he never loved. Rent the film. The romance holds. The poster does not have to print any of that.

He couldn't afford a plane ticket, so he sold everything he owned, bought a bicycle and left Delhi in January 1977. By May he was in Europe, and he took the train the rest of the way to Gothenburg. The poster says 6,000 miles by bike. "I didn't just travel by bicycle. I got rides with trucks," he said in 2017.

"I was cycling for love, but never loved cycling. It's simple."
