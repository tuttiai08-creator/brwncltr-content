## Proposed headline

Naisha said *911* is completely hers. Skrillex is on the bill, not the byline.

## Story type

Feature (dominant), with a news lede. The news is the 7 August 2026 three-track debut *911* (Desi Trill / Interscope). The feature is that she is the author — named languages, attributed heritage, producer roles as printed — not a Skrillex-protégé recap and not a rewrite of Homegrown’s 11 August club-lens piece.

## Target audience

South Asian and diaspora readers in the US, Canada, and UK who already follow Interscope / Desi Trill and Hindi-language club records, and readers who met Naisha only as a name on Skrillex credits. Not “everyone,” and not a generic EDM audience.

## Why now

Today is Tuesday 1 September 2026. *911* is dated **7 August 2026** on Apple’s catalog — four weeks, still a first-cycle window, not a recap of a year-old record. Homegrown published a club-lens feature on 11 August. The peg is the dated debut plus her on-record claim that the project is hers, not a second review of the bass.

## Central angle

Naisha is the author of *911*. She said the songs people already knew were “only a glimpse,” and that this one “feels completely mine.” Apple’s storefront lists the same three tracks as *911 - Single*. Label-adjacent copy names English, Hindi, and Caribbean patois, and Trinidadian heritage on her mother’s side. In the interview this packet opened, she did not say “Trinidad.” She did name India. ## Full draft

Today is Tuesday 1 September 2026. On 7 August, Apple’s catalog dated a three-track object by Naisha: *911 - Single*, “3 songs, 7 minutes,” copyright **Desi Trill Music, under exclusive license to Interscope Records**. Clash, uDiscover, GroundSounds, Homegrown, and EE72 all call the same object her debut EP. This piece does not pick a winner between “EP” and “Single.” It is three tracks. It is four weeks old. It is not a year-old recap.

The first track, *so then*, billed on iTunes as Naisha & Ty Dolla $ign, already had a single date of 1 July 2026 (Spotify: 2 minutes 32 seconds, same Desi Trill / Interscope line). Track two is *CONTRABAND*, billed **Naisha, Skrillex, BEAM & Dilip**, 1 minute 51 seconds, explicit. Track three is *jaana*, billed to Naisha alone, 2 minutes 58 seconds. That order is Apple’s. Homegrown’s 11 August feature listed *CONTRABAND* first in a dek. The rights-holder listing is the one this desk uses for sequence.

Homegrown, on 11 August at 12:00 pm, already wrote the club-lens piece: Hindi vocals, Trinidadian patois, “heavy low-end club weight,” a “refusal to sanitise.” That last phrase is Homegrown’s frame. It is not a fact about audiences, and it is not this article. The production add is narrower. She said the record was hers. The famous names on the credits are still credits.

### “Completely mine”

Clash printed her statement in full after “She comments…” Other pages reprint the same block, with light punctuation changes.

“For the last few years, people have heard me through collaborations and features, but those songs were only a glimpse into my world,” she said. “With ‘911’, I finally get to share a project that feels completely mine. My melodies, my influences, my perspective start to finish.”

“Executive produced by Naisha, sonically and visually.”

“Visual storytelling is such a big part of how I express myself. It’s my way of translating who I am and the beautiful chaos I exist in. The same chaos lives in my music too.”

“‘911’ is authentically me. Every part of it. I am excited to be at a stage where I can make things imperfectly perfect and trust myself enough to put them out into the world.”

“Just people making magic because they love music.”

“‘911’ is emotional, chaotic, playful, honest and completely my world.”

“So call 911”

“Naisha is here”

uDiscover cuts the same thought for the drop: “With 911, I finally get to share a project that feels completely mine,” and “emotional, chaotic, playful, honest and completely my world.” MixTape Madness, filing the *so then* single on 1 July, adds a paragraph Clash did not print: “so then,” my first single, started as a hook I recorded ages ago. One day it came to life in the most organic way with my producers. Even Ty becoming part of it happened naturally. He heard the song, connected with it, and wanted to be part of it. Those are the moments I live for.”

On 6 August, the day before the three-track date, Erica Rana’s EE72 interview put the collaborators in her voice, not as the story’s subject. “Skrillex is one of the executive producers on the EP alongside Dilip Venkatesh and me,” she said. “More than that, they are family to me.” She calls them Sonny and Dilip. “I have been on a lot of Sonny’s music, and he has been part of almost everything I have made too.” Ty Dolla $ign, in the same interview: “He came by the studio with Poo Bear to hang. It was maybe 2 AM, and I was working on so then. He jumped on it because the energy felt right and we were having fun.” That is her session story. MixTape Madness’ reporter line that *So Then* was “produced by Skrillex” is on the same internet. This draft does not reconcile them.

The title, to her, is not a US emergency number with a novelty sticker. “For me, 911 is more of a feeling than an emergency number,” she told EE72. “By the time this project was finished, getting it out felt like a matter of life or death. I couldn’t keep it inside anymore. Even though 911 is American, it has become a symbol that people everywhere understand. I have always felt like a child of the world, so it felt right to take something so globally recognised and make it personal.” She also said she wanted listeners hungry: “This project is just the beginning. I want them to know more is coming, and this is just the appetizer from my side.”

### India on the record. Trinidad on the PR.

A named-heritage profile that flattened this into “desi pop” would be the wrong piece. The sources do not give one word.

What she told EE72 about the house she grew up in is specific. “My mama used to sing on the radio in India when she was younger, and my grandmother made sure I learned about Hindustani classical music.” Her mother’s records, she said, were Whitney Houston, Fantasia Barrino, Asha Bhosle, Grace Jones, Bob Marley and the Wailers, ABBA. “My dad loved Qawwali, so I grew up hearing Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan, Guru Dutt and Geeta Dutt all the time.” She taught herself Logic during Covid. She does not, in that interview, say Trinidad. She does not say “mother’s side.” She does say “my country,” in a sentence about drama and Bollywood, and “I have always felt like a child of the world.”

What uDiscover printed, in the reporter’s voice, is the line the drop copy repeats: “Across the EP, Naisha moves between English, Hindi and Caribbean patois, reflecting both her Indian background and Trinidadian heritage on her mother’s side.” Drop The Spotlight and The Garnette Report reprint the same “nod to her Trinidadian heritage on her mother’s side.” GroundSounds, the same week, writes “Trinidadian and Indian heritage” without “mother’s side.” Clash’s reporter, filing the same drop, writes only “Indian heritage — she predominantly sings in Hindi,” and does not print Trinidad or patois in the fetched body.

Those pages are not nothing. They are also not her saying it. Homegrown’s 11 August feature roots her in “Trinidadian and Indian heritage” and in Mumbai, Delhi, New York, and London — Homegrown’s geography, not a quote. Homegrown’s April 2025 Ultra recap called her “Odisha-born.” That word is not in EE72 and is not in the 2026 PR reprints this packet used. It is not in this piece.

Languages stay named as the pages name them. uDiscover and the PR reprints: English, Hindi, Caribbean patois. Clash: predominantly Hindi. The official *CONTRABAND* video’s YouTube auto-captions — auto, not a lyric booklet — print “SHE AH CONTRABAND,” “KUD LE KUD LE,” and “GOT HIM SINGING 10 RAAGS.” That is caption evidence of mixed diction on the official upload. It is not a certified translation, and it is not proof of how an audience heard it.

PR/bio copy also calls Hindi her “native” language on *Diwali* and *so then*. That sentence is bio copy. This draft does not put “native Hindi” in her mouth.

Clash calls her a “Mumbai raised artist.” The PR sentence used by Garnette, Drop The Spotlight, Voir, and GroundSounds is “Born and raised in India and now based between Los Angeles and Mumbai.” EE72’s reporter, and Interview Magazine’s lede as returned in search, put the 4 a.m. Skrillex meeting in **London**. Clash and the PR block say “a rave at 4 a.m.” without a city. London is attributed to those interviews, not silently written into the PR.

### The song is called *CONTRABAND*

uDiscover: the video “was filmed in Mumbai and developed with creative direction from Naisha. Ruby and Colin Tilley directed the video, which follows Naisha through city streets, crowds and choreographed dance sequences.” GroundSounds, dated 7 August in the URL, has the same directors, “filmed on location across her native India,” then “Filmed across Mumbai.” The official upload, on NaishaVEVO, is titled “Naisha - CONTRABAND (feat. Skrillex, BEAM & Dilip) [Official Music Video].” uDiscover’s metaphor — “the titular contraband” — is a caption on a music video. It is not a customs case.

Who did what on that track is printed several ways. iTunes and the VEVO title put Skrillex, BEAM, and Dilip on the artist line. Homegrown says *CONTRABAND* was “Co-produced alongside Skrillex and Dilip, and featuring BEAM.” Clash says it “features her old friend Skrillex,” and does not name BEAM or Dilip there. DMY says it was “produced with longtime collaborator Skrillex.” Garnette’s later *jaana* visualizer note calls Skrillex and Dilip Venkatesh “co-executive producers” of *911*. Her EE72 sentence is the one this desk uses for the EP: executive producers are Skrillex, Dilip Venkatesh, and her. The rest of the Dilip question — exec versus track producer versus featured artist — stays a conflict. Guessing would be the error.

Homegrown and DMY also write that Skrillex has been playing *CONTRABAND* in live sets. This packet did not find a setlist or a clip. Ultra Music Festival in March 2025, per uDiscover and Homegrown’s 2 April 2025 recap, was *Talk To Me*, not *CONTRABAND*. The live-set line is a circulating claim. It is not used here as a fact.

### What this desk did not do

It did not sit with the full seven minutes as a critic. Apple’s CDN served 30-second AAC previews of all three tracks on 1 September. YouTube blocked a download of the official video. Auto-captions were retrieved for *CONTRABAND* only. Clash’s “towering vocal” on *jaana*, Homegrown’s “sub-bass, UK club heat,” and DMY’s “heavy low end of dubstep” are those publications’ sentences. They are not this reporter’s ears. A draft that reviewed *911* as if it had been heard end to end would be a rules violation. This one does not.

It did not rewrite Homegrown. That piece already described campaign-banner artwork — Homegrown prints the slogans “EAST OR WEST, NAISHA IS THE BEST” and “CLEAN NAISHA, CLEAN INDIA,” and names badges for Skrillex, Ty Dolla $ign, BEAM, and M1OnTheBeat. If a human wants the cover as a visual, the image is still to be checked. This prose does not build a second club essay out of Homegrown’s metaphor.

It did not open Instagram as a primary. Homegrown credits an Instagram still; EE72 prints @naishabhargabi. That is not a virality metric.

People already knew her, if they knew her, from other people’s records. uDiscover’s recap, which this desk is not going to re-report as a discography feature, lists *Talk To Me* (November 2024, with Champion, Four Tet, and Skrillex), Ultra Miami in March 2025, *GULAB XX* in 2025, and *Cheeni* and *Diwali* on Skrillex’s *SOMA* in June 2026. Those are the “glimpse” she was talking about. They are not *911*.

### What this is not

It is not a Skrillex story with a vocalist attached. She said the project was completely hers. Apple bills her as the collection artist.

It is not “desi pop.” Hindi, English, and Caribbean patois are the words on uDiscover. India is the country in her EE72 sentences. Trinidadian heritage on her mother’s side is the phrase on uDiscover and the PR reprints, not a quote we found from her.

It is not a crime story. The song is called *CONTRABAND*.

It is not a craft review of an album this desk did not fully hear.

It is not proof that Skrillex has been playing the record in sets. Homegrown and DMY said so. A setlist did not.

It is not Homegrown again.

Four weeks after a 7 August date, the publishable facts are smaller than a breakout recap wants. A three-track debut is on Interscope via Desi Trill. She put her name on the executive-producer line next to Skrillex and Dilip Venkatesh, and then said the songs were hers start to finish. The languages on the one-sheet are named. The Caribbean side of the bio is still, on the pages checked today, the one-sheet. That is enough to write a first-cycle profile. It is not enough to hand the byline to anyone else.
