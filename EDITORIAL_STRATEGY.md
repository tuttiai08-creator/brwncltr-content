# Editorial strategy — brwncltr

brwncltr covers **South Asian culture and media** with attention to people living in the region **and** in diaspora. Coverage should help readers understand what is happening, why it matters in cultural context, and what is distinct about specific communities—not a generic “South Asian” blur.

Editorial value and cultural relevance outrank chasing traffic. A quiet story with a clear audience and a real angle can beat a viral item we would only rewrite.

---

## Major editorial areas

These are **beats**, not rigid silos. A piece may sit in more than one; pick a **primary** beat for categorization and a honest secondary if needed.

### South Asian culture

Practices, language, food, ritual, art, literature, regional histories, and everyday culture **named specifically** (country, region, language, community) when that specificity is the point. Culture is not only festivals and food; it includes how people argue, create, mourn, and organize.

### Diaspora life and identity

How South Asian people live, work, parent, date, worship, and belong **outside** a single “home” frame—or between multiple homes. Immigration, citizenship, class, caste (when reported carefully and sourced), colorism, language loss/retention, and intergenerational tension belong here when they are the story, not a stereotype checklist.

### Entertainment

Celebrity, industry, fandom, awards, tours, and the business of attention—Bollywood, Lollywood, Kollywood, Tollywood, Pakistani and Bangladeshi screen industries, independent film, theater, comedy, and crossover Hollywood/streaming—**when the South Asian or diaspora stake is real**.

### Music / film / television

Releases, performances, craft, criticism, and industry context. Distinguish **review**, **reported feature**, **explainer**, and **news**. Soundtrack culture, independent scenes, and diaspora artists are in scope when we can add context, not only a plot recap.

### Business / creators / entrepreneurship

Founders, creators, media companies, nightlife and hospitality, beauty and CPG, and the economies around fandom and platforms. Prefer stories with a **specific company, person, product, or policy**—not vague “South Asians are killing it” roundups unless the roundup has a documented, useful frame.

### Fashion / lifestyle (where relevant)

Style, beauty, design, and living culture when they connect to South Asian makers, wearers, markets, or diaspora taste—not generic trend copy. Relevance test: would this exist the same way without South Asian creators or audiences? If yes, it is probably not ours unless the angle is explicitly comparative and sourced.

### Internet / current conversations

Memes, platform fights, discourse, and “everyone is talking about” moments **only** when we can (a) verify what actually happened and (b) add context, history, or a diaspora/regional reading. Viral is not the same as true or worth our name.

### Community stories

Local and community-scale work: neighborhoods, student groups, religious and cultural institutions, mutual aid, sports clubs, language schools, and people who are not already famous. These pieces often have high editorial value and lower “trend” scores; that is acceptable.

## Story types

Use one **primary** type per piece. Do not disguise opinion as news.

| Type | Job | Evidence bar |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **News** | What happened, who, when, where, what is confirmed | High: dated, attributed, preferably primary |
| **Analysis** | What the facts imply; competing explanations | Facts attributed; interpretation labeled as analysis |
| **Explainer** | How something works or how we got here | Definitions, timeline, sourced background |
| **Feature** | Scene, character, reported narrative | Reporting notes, on-the-record or clearly attributed material |
| **List / guide** | Practical or curated utility | Each item checkable; criteria stated |
| **Opinion** | An argued view | Labeled opinion; still no invented facts; distinguish the writer’s view from reported claims |

If a draft mixes types, the template’s **story type** field should name the **dominant** form and note the mix in the angle.

## What “worth covering” means here

A story is a candidate when **most** of the following are true:

- It is **culturally or communally meaningful** to South Asian and/or diaspora readers, not only adjacent.
- brwncltr can offer a **useful angle**: context, specificity, primary voices, or a frame other outlets skipped—not a paraphrase of a larger publication.
- **Sources exist** or can be sought without fabricating access.
- The piece will still make sense after the first news cycle **or** it is honestly **time-sensitive** and we can ship while it is still true.

A story is a weak fit when:

- The only hook is search volume or someone else’s headline.
- It treats “South Asian” as one culture, one politics, or one diaspora.
- It needs controversy we cannot source.
- We would be late, thinner, and less informed than existing coverage with nothing to add.

## Voice and stance (defaults until the owner overrides)

- Curious, specific, and adult. Not breathless, not academic-opaque, not brand-safe emptiness.
- Name countries, languages, and communities. Do not default to India-as-proxy for the region.
- Respect religion and irreligion without sermon or sneer.
- Celebrity coverage should still be **accurate**; intimacy is not invented.
- Humor is allowed; punching down at communities is not.

Owner should eventually supply **sample published pieces** and a **do-not-cover** list so agents can calibrate. Until then, agents default to caution: pass on thin celebrity gossip, unsourced caste/religion claims, and medical/legal advice.

---

## Audience (working definition)

Readers who care about South Asian cultural life and media—including people in South Asia, first- and later-generation diaspora, mixed families, and non-South Asian readers who want **non-extractive, specific** coverage. Agents must not assume a single city, class, or country. The owner should later name **priority geographies** (e.g. which diaspora hubs) for discovery weighting.
